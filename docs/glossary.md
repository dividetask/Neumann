# Glossary

- **Approach arrow** — The arrow dragged out from the ship on the map to set
  the approach vector: direction and entry speed.
- **Approach vector** — The direction and speed at which the player's ship
  enters a star system.
- **Auto factory** — The ship's on-board factory; it turns mined minerals
  into repairs.
- **Auto-pause event** — Something that stops a time skip before its time is
  up.
- **Bearing only** — A contact whose direction is known but not its
  distance.
- **Bogey** — An enemy ship or torpedo: something that moves on its own, so
  once seen its position can only be estimated. Not slugs, fixed defences,
  or bases.
- **Burn** — Firing the ship's engine to change its velocity.
- **Claimed** — A star system with every enemy ship, fixed defence, and base
  destroyed.
- **Computer** — The module that runs a ship; without it the ship is dead.
- **Contact** — Any object the ship's sensors have detected.
- **Daemon core** — The simplest computer: a primitive daemon process, run by
  raiders, fixed defences, and bases.
- **Dead** — A ship whose computer is destroyed: it drifts and does nothing,
  and is as good as destroyed.
- **Dead reckoning** — Carrying a contact's last position fix forward to
  now under gravity, assuming it coasts.
- **Design** — A ship's list of modules; fixed defences and bases are designs
  too.
- **Desired distance** — How far a burn should take the ship before it
  brakes to a stop.
- **Desired speed** — The speed a burn should reach before the ship coasts.
- **Direction circles** — The YAW and PITCH dials used to pick a direction
  relative to the ship.
- **Emission receiver** — A passive sensor that detects active sensors.
- **Emitting** — Using an active sensor (radar or lidar); enemy emission
  receivers can detect it.
- **Estimate** — A contact's sensed position and velocity, with its errors.
- **Estimated position** — Where a contact probably is now, by dead
  reckoning, with a sphere showing where it could be.
- **Facing** — Which way a ship's front and top point.
- **Fixed defence** — A weapon installation without real engines; it only
  keeps its orbit.
- **Focus range** — How far a laser keeps its beam tight enough for full
  damage.
- **Ghost** — A faint marker where a bogey was last seen, once its fix is
  too old for an estimate.
- **Hit point** — One megajoule of damage a module can absorb.
- **Jinking** — Evasive manoeuvres: random sideways pushes from the
  thrusters that spoil cannon fire.
- **Lidar** — Laser ranging: an active sensor that tracks the target very
  precisely.
- **Light delay** — The time light takes to reach the ship; sensors show
  contacts as they were, not as they are.
- **Material** — What modules are made of (iron, aluminium, and so on), in
  tonnes; mined and used by the factory later.
- **Mineral estimate** — The predicted mineral resources at a destination
  star, shown when choosing where to go.
- **Missile bay** — A module that holds and launches torpedoes.
- **Module** — One component of a ship, with a cost, weight, and
  manufacturing time.
- **Pending** — Designed or suggested, but held back for a later version.
- **Radar** — An active microwave sensor that finds hot and cold objects.
- **Remaining** — How many enemy ships, fixed defences, and bases the
  player has seen and not yet destroyed.
- **Seed** — The number a random star system is generated from; the same
  seed gives the same system.
- **Seen position** — Where the instruments last placed a contact, as of
  when its light left it.
- **Sentient core** — The probe's computer: a sentient AI, the player.
- **Sensor rules** — The radar and lidar on/off switches.
- **Ship state** — Damage, fuel, and ammunition, kept between battles.
- **Signature** — How easy an object is for telescopes to see; higher while
  burning.
- **Sublight** — Slower than light; no ship in the game exceeds light speed.
- **Slug inbound** — A detected slug heading for the ship.
- **Telescope** — A passive infrared/optical sensor.
- **Time skip** — A player control that moves the game forward by a set time.
- **Turn rate** — How fast a ship can change its facing.
- **Uncertainty** — The size of a contact's position error.
- **Von Neumann probe** — A self-repairing ship that mines and manufactures
  what it needs. The player's ship is one.
- **Weapon rules** — Standing orders for when weapons fire on their own.
